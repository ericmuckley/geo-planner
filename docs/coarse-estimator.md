# Coarse nationwide estimator

Implemented October 22, 2026 as the first slice of the user's product reset: a simple fullstack Vercel app estimating long isochrones from arbitrary contiguous-US land origins, with refinement later. The main page defaults to this model at two hours. A 30-minute–12-hour slider previews the duration while moving and automatically estimates on release; the optional compute button handles coordinate edits/retry. The connected provider and detailed regional experiment remain available separately.

## What the model does

The TypeScript server searches a **0.025° geographic grid** over `[-125,24,-66,50]`. Cells are about **1.8–2.8 km** across, depending on latitude and direction. This is display resolution, not a measured spatial or travel-time error bound. A land mask limits general travel to the US; lake cells are removed. Natural Earth's generalized US road lines and Census 2026 primary/secondary roads add faster, bidirectional links between neighboring cells.

| Travel | Assumed speed |
| --- | --- |
| General land travel, including land without roads | 25 km/h |
| Major highway | 105 km/h |
| Beltway | 90 km/h |
| Bypass | 90 km/h |
| Secondary highway | 80 km/h |
| Other retained road | 55 km/h |

The current **v8** bounded Dijkstra search rebuilds the finer graph while retaining v7’s rule of a separate Census road state in each supported cell. Base states use the fastest/longest retained Natural Earth visit, a land center or a checked mixed-shore representative. Census states use the existing supplemental visit anchor. Source arcs use assumed S1100=105 / S1200=80 km/h, preserving bends and a physical chord lower bound, with continuous-land connectors to Census anchors at 25 km/h. A road can continue on this layer instead of visiting the unrelated base anchor at every cell. Switching layers pays continuous-land physical distance at 25 km/h, rounded upward to at least 0.1 second; Census states have no implicit overland neighbors. All base routes/input options remain available. Original NE links retain their legacy class-speed connector assumption. One selected road position per layer still merges nearby roads/grade-separated crossings and can distort time. Interior base overland diagonals require both adjacent orthogonal land cells; explicit local shore links require continuous generalized land. Road-only water cells permit explicit source bridge alignments. These are coarse assumptions, not a routable topology.

Origin validation uses the generalized US boundary and lake polygons with a **1 km boundary tolerance**. The preserved v3 rule attaches an input to its representative by continuous generalized land when possible. If that fails, checked nearby land representatives and retained-road access points provide fallback attachments at 25 km/h. Each v3 road access point follows the road visit that owns its canonical anchor, permitting explicit bridge/tunnel access without inventing a direct water crossing. V4 also considers projections/endpoints on the owning source visit in the input’s cell, paying checked local distance at 25 km/h and polyline road distance at its class speed. V7 also attaches directly to the Census state using the same checked/paid visit access, alongside the original base-anchor attachment. The minimum-time attachment per state seeds search; direct/v3 fallback access remains available. `attachmentKm` is the distance of the lowest-time retained seed, not necessarily the one used by every reached path. Every supported fallback seeds the search; the nearest may be an isolated land patch. An accepted input without supported access displays its own isolated cell. Shared land/traversal masks are not changed by the query. Filled MultiPolygons take the minimum arrival across a cell’s states and trace whole reached display cells, including road-supported land samples and 10,936 mixed-shore representatives on generalized US land outside lakes, preserving holes and disconnected components. The extra display cells do not gain general travel across water; contours still fill whole cells and can extend into water or across a border. Small isolated islands can return only their local land cells for every budget. One representative per cell still merges or omits smaller land components; whole-cell shading is approximate. Ferries and international crossings are excluded; US-only source features and a coarse mask do not constitute a precise border-access model.

The endpoint accepts **30–720 whole minutes**. It returns explicit approximation warnings, model/source versions, reached-cell count, origin attachment distance and separate grid-search/contour timings. `coverageVerified` remains false. Shading does not establish an accessible road at every point. Traffic, one-way roads, turns, terrain, gates and actual access are not modeled. The regional comparisons below guide these assumptions; national travel-time accuracy remains unvalidated.

Natural Earth sources are the roads, countries and lakes GeoJSON files in the official Natural Earth repository at tag **v5.1.2**. Census inputs are all 48 contiguous-state partitions plus DC from the [2026 TIGER/Line primary/secondary-road release](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/geo/tiger-line-file.html), with 49 source locks in the artifact and [national source evidence](evidence/coarse-national-2026-10-22/sources.json). This linework is not a routable topology. The [Census technical guidance](https://www2.census.gov/geo/pdfs/maps-data/data/tiger/tgrshp2026/TGRSHP2026_TechDoc_Ch1.pdf) permits reproduction of government data, requests source credit and a conspicuous repackaging statement, and identifies TIGER/Line as a registered Census trademark. API metadata and visible notices credit both sources and explain that Geo Planner modifies/repackages the data into its own coarse estimates; Census has not reviewed or endorsed them.

The artifact is a normal source file intended for Git and deployment. The 68.8 MB Natural Earth raw inputs live in ignored `data/coarse-sources/natural-earth-v5.1.2/`; Census inputs total 289.2 MB under `data/coarse-sources/tiger-2026/`. The offline resolution/road-state builders use these pinned sources and the checked diagnostic reader, never at runtime. [Local development](local-development.md#regenerate-the-coarse-grid) gives exact downloads and build commands.

`GET/POST /api/estimate` runs inside the existing TanStack Start/Nitro app, including the generated Vercel handler. It needs no database, Python, Docker, local worker, routing-service key or network fetch. Decoded arrays are reused within a warm process; each query owns its labels and contour mask. The browser still downloads its basemap independently. Database-backed saved history is now implemented as a separate API without moving estimation into PostgreSQL or introducing PostGIS; see [saved-history setup](local-development.md#saved-history-api). The original evidence below precedes that follow-up, which is recorded in [current implementation status](implementation-status.md#saved-history-verification).

## V3 general shore connectivity

V3 applies the same geography/road rules throughout the contiguous-US grid. There are **no place names, example coordinates or regional exceptions in the production rules**. Selected trips live only in validation. Speeds remain unchanged from v2.

The builder finds land within mixed cells using the retained country/lake geometry and adds local links only between representatives on continuous land in the same country polygon component. It partitions segments at every polygon intersection, so even narrow water gaps are checked rather than skipped by sampling. Separate islands touching at a corner do not become connected. Whole-cell shading and the inherited interior overland grid remain approximate; this is not exact coastline clipping or a full access/terrain model.

Input access uses the same land check, with at most 1 km of water only at the initial input end to match shore tolerance; it cannot ignore later water gaps. Where straight land attachment fails, 245 source-derived road access points follow the actual retained road visit to its existing anchor. These let bridge-connected islands join the road network. Fallback attachment considers all supported candidates in the input cell/eight neighbors, charging each local/retained-road access distance at 25 km/h. `attachmentKm` reports the minimum initial attachment distance; different reached paths may use another supported attachment. Unsupported inputs stay isolated rather than acquiring a water shortcut. Point comparisons use these same attachments at both ends.

On the saved eleven routes, approximate point costs are unchanged (31.381m mean difference from the reference). On the **same 23 common-finite regional cases**, mean difference changes **37.730m v2 → 36.893m v3**: two improve, 21 stay unchanged, none regress. The previously unreached **24th** destination is now reachable and shaded from the opposite shore at 6h/12h. Its modeled point cost is **146.0m versus a 61.3m reference**, reported separately from common-case means. This closes a membership gap, not the missing-road/time-accuracy gap. [Comparisons, failed prototypes, tests and packaging](evidence/coarse-v3-2026-10-22/README.md).

A strict land-only attachment prototype stranded New York and Key West; a nearest-only fallback still stranded Key West on a small land patch. Both attempts are preserved. Explicit retained-road access plus multiple fallback seeds restore those origins without allowing unrelated islands to cross channels. All **42 national smoke cases** pass, with new checks against degenerate connected-origin results. Key West’s 6h cell count changes 344 → 338, while 10h/12h increase: source-access paths and display membership changed, so this is an explained model observation, not an accuracy guarantee.
